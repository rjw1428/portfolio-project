Assembly sequence

Eight steps, in order. Each one taught me something the next one needed.

Step 01 · {{career.norfolk.company}} · 2013

Mechanical supervisor, locomotive works

I supervised production lines at the locomotive works in Altoona, PA — and got tired of paper travelers. So I taught myself to program on the shop floor: a multi-tier data system tracking work across the production line, and Python automation pulling QC measurements straight off the CNC machines — eliminating eight hours a week of manual data entry.

Self-taught, on the clock · −8 hrs/wk manual entry · Jan 2013 – Jul 2015

Step 02 · NAVSEA SWRMC, U.S. Navy · 2015

Engineering technician, San Diego waterfront

Engineering Technician V at the Southwest Regional Maintenance Center — where the software kept outrunning the day job. I built a MERN-stack data collection system that cut data processing time and automated stakeholder reporting, and it earned a U.S. Navy Innovation Award.

U.S. Navy Innovation Award · MERN stack · Aug 2015 – May 2018

Step 03 · {{career.ssr.role}} · c. 2016

SSR Digital Displays

Nights and weekends of the San Diego years: I founded a digital menu-board startup — real-time boards a bar owner updates from any web-connected device. Installed in five venues. Sales, installs, and every line of code — mine.

Field-installed: 5 venues · San Diego + Philadelphia

Step 04 · {{career.accenture.company}} · 2018

Senior analyst, Life Science Cloud

Big codebases, bigger stakeholder maps. Working with Life Science Cloud clients, I ran discovery on their document-upload pain, then shipped the fixes and automation myself — cutting client-reported issues 84% and saving roughly $45k a year. Sitting with the customer first became the habit.

−84% client-reported issues · ~$45k/yr saved · May 2018 – Apr 2019

Step 05 · Comcast X1 telemetry · 2019

Front-end team lead, real-time telemetry

Leading three developers, I re-architected the real-time telemetry front end around NgRx — resolving 87% of the tech debt and tripling feature delivery. I built the JavaScript data-collection library that scaled to 30 million devices, D3.js visualizations including a live per-market map of the U.S., and Python blue/green monitoring to catch data drift. Instrumenting heavy machinery, if you squint — just with better tooling.

30M devices · 87% tech debt resolved · +200% feature delivery · Apr 2019 – May 2021

Step 06 · {{career.fort-robotics.company}} · 2021

Senior engineer, front-end team lead

I led a team of five building Fort's first client-facing product: an Angular web and Ionic mobile portal for managing their robotic safety-controller platform — the release that let a hardware company pivot to platform-first software. I machined the core component library myself: infinite-scroll and lazy-load directives, form wizards, async validators.

Angular · Ionic · strict TypeScript · RxJS · team of 5 · 2021–2022

Step 07 · {{career.xumo.company}} · Xumo TV · 2022

Principal engineer, technical lead — smart-TV app

Technical lead on the resident smart-TV application — the layer where client-facing UI, third-party apps, physical TV hardware, and external APIs all meet on one screen. I built TV Toolkit, a one-click debugging and device-interface suite for developers and QA that cut onboarding and dev cycles 10%. My AI Academy capstone shipped an XGBoost model into a customer-facing troubleshooting chat app, raising next-action acceptance 30% — and I was promoted to Principal Engineer along the way.

TV Toolkit: −10% dev cycles · XGBoost: +30% acceptance · May 2022 – 2025

Step 08 · {{career.x1.company}} · X1 · AS BUILT

Principal engineer — AI technical lead, X1 platform

The current revision. I lead a squad embedded across the X1 org: we run discovery to find where developers actually lose time, then ship AI workflows that get adopted company-wide. An agentic vulnerability-fix pipeline with human-in-the-loop PRs has cut open security vulnerabilities 80%. A spec-driven, AI-assisted migration is moving a mission-critical app from Java 8 to Java 25 — literally working from the drawings. An AI system repairs broken tests, finds E2E coverage gaps, and generates new tests; an org-wide LLM onboarding tool puts the same leverage in non-technical hands.

−80% open security vulns · adopted company-wide · May 2025 – present
